1. The Governance Trap

Every data governance program eventually hits the same wall. Centralize too hard and the governance team becomes the bottleneck for every schema change and access request. Decentralize too far and domains route around the bottleneck — producing uncatalogued pipelines, duplicated logic, and metrics nobody can audit. Most organizations swing between the two and never land on a model that holds.

Why the order matters

Deploying PBAC, drift monitoring, and federated councils simultaneously causes operational paralysis. Organizational foundations (Tier 1) earn the executive mandate that makes Tier 2's technical mapping possible, which in turn makes Tier 3's automation safe to turn on.

5. Why "As Code" Matters

The Typical Governance Deliverable
  • •Static PDF charter: approved once, re-read rarely, drifts from reality within a quarter.
  • •Documented access rules: enforcement depends on people remembering and following them manually.
  • •Re-engagement required: every new domain needs a fresh consulting pass to extend the model.
The Governance-as-Code Deliverable
  • •Versioned YAML charters & RACI: reviewable in pull requests, changes tracked like any other code change.
  • •OPA/Rego policies with automated tests: access control and data contract enforcement actually run in CI, not just in a policy document.
  • •Forkable at handover: the client's own platform team extends the same repo to onboard the next domain at a fraction of the original cost.

6. Not a Catalog Tool — and Day-One on Databricks or Fabric

A governance catalog (Collibra, Purview, Atlan, Immuta) is infrastructure — it gives you a place to register metadata and define policies. It doesn't decide your operating model, write your charters, or author your first contracts for you. The Accelerator is the engagement that installs the operating model and hands over working code — and it's catalog-agnostic, so it doesn't compete with whatever registry the client already owns or is buying.
